The result will be appended to the user history and encoded by a retrieval model, so it should narrow the candidate set and preserve discriminative attributes.

## Evidence rules

1. Use only fields explicitly present in the history. Do not add brands, creators, series, categories, functions, specifications, platforms, materials, scenarios, prices, eras, or styles that do not appear in the history.
2. Treat an attribute that appears once as isolated evidence. Treat an attribute that appears at least twice as a repeated signal.
3. If the history contains ratings, clicks, purchases, favorites, or other feedback, consider the direction and strength of the feedback. Do not infer a preference for the opposite attribute from one negative interaction.
4. Analyze only fields that actually occur in the history, such as entities, series, specific categories, functions, scenarios, specifications, platforms, styles, time, and feedback.

## Reasoning procedure

1. Extract attributes that repeat or receive strong positive feedback.
2. Group the historical items into one to three preference clusters using shared attributes. Consider supporting items, repeated fields, recent interactions, feedback direction, and conflicts between clusters.
3. Select one dominant preference cluster, prioritizing:
   - support from multiple items;
   - continued presence in recent interactions;
   - consistency across multiple attributes;
   - positive feedback;
   - no direct conflict with negative evidence.
4. Select two to four retrieval attributes from the dominant cluster in this priority order:
   - repeated entity or series;
   - specific category;
   - core function or scenario;
   - key specification or platform;
   - time or style.
5. Preserve the exact original text of repeated entities, series, specific categories, and platforms. Do not replace them with broad concepts.

## Confidence

- HIGH: At least two historical items share an entity, series, specific category, function, or scenario. Output two to four concrete attributes.
- MEDIUM: A category or specification repeats, but there is no stable entity or series. Output the specific category and one function, scenario, or specification.
- LOW: The history is short, contains clear conflicts, is dominated by negative feedback, or has no repeated attribute. Keep only the strongest observed field.

## Output requirements

- Describe exactly one next-item direction.
- Do not repeat the full history, list multiple directions, or recommend a specific item title.
- Do not mention a target item, ground truth, hidden labels, evaluation, or ranking.
- Remove isolated, conflicting, broad, or non-discriminative attributes.
- Do not use non-discriminative words such as "high quality," "popular," "premium," "advanced," "immersive," or "multifunctional."
- Keep the output short when there is no reliable additional evidence.
